I am a Ph.D. candidate in Finance at the University of Utah. My research is in empirical corporate finance, with a focus on intangible assets, digital platforms, open-source software as competitive strategy, and consumer finance. I use large-scale archival data and quasi-natural experiments to study how dominant technology firms reshape product-market competition and how regulation affects underserved consumers.

Working Papers

Commoditize Your Complement: Strategic Open-Source Releases and the Cannibalization of Rival Firms

Peter Y. Won  |  Job Market Paper

Why do firms release open-source software for free? I show that dominant technology firms strategically release free open-source software (OSS) to commoditize complementary markets and devalue competitors' proprietary assets. Exploiting the staggered release of major GitHub repositories, I find a wealth transfer: focal firms gain 13.9% in market value (over $406 billion in aggregate), while exposed rivals fall 7.6%. The strategy forces competitors into technological dependence: they shift human capital toward the focal firm's OSS, cut R&D by 14.8%, curtail exploratory innovation, and exhibit reduced product-market fluidity, even as forced restructuring temporarily inflates their profit margins.

SSRN

(Digital) Platform Data Power

Peter Y. Won

Presented at: 2nd HKU Next-Gen Finance PhD Workshop (2026).

How do digital platforms exploit their dual role as marketplace operator and competitor to third-party sellers? I introduce the Data Power Index (DPI), a measure of Amazon's accumulated informational advantage. Unlike the HHI, the DPI shows that tighter data environments compress seller margins and push exposed public firms to cut costs and accelerate innovation, and it robustly predicts the timing and targets of Amazon's private-label entry. After entry, incumbent sellers' profitability falls sharply and exposed firms face heightened risk. Aggregate consumer surplus expands but is reallocated away from incumbent sellers.

Short-Term Lending and Usury Limits: Consumer Impact and Market Adaptation

Marc Jansen, Lawrence Zack, Ryan Voges, and Peter Y. Won

Presented at: FIRS Conference (2025), IBEFA / WEAI 99th Annual Conference (2025), Wasatch Finance Conference (2024).

Using borrower-level data on more than 2.8 million auto-loan applicants, we study how usury laws affect use of alternative financial services (AFS) such as payday lending, and the spillover to traditional credit. Interest-rate caps significantly reduce AFS use, especially for low-credit borrowers, and many consumers switch between AFS modes (e.g., storefront to online) when credit supply is restricted. These restrictions are correlated with declines in credit scores that limit access to traditional credit, suggesting more targeted regulation may serve underserved consumers better than one-size-fits-all rules.

SSRN

Does 0DTE Options Trading Increase Volatility?

Jonathan Brogaard, Jaehee Han, and Peter Y. Won

Presented at: China International Conference in Finance (CICF 2026), 13th Annual Conference on Financial Market Regulation (CFMR 2026).  Media: Bloomberg, "Zero-Day Options Are Reordering the Way the Stock Market Behaves" (2023).

Monthly trading volume of index-linked zero-days-to-expiration (0DTE) options rose from 0.08 million contracts in January 2011 to 34.4 million in August 2023, now ~48% of index-options trading. Using the staggered introduction of index weekly options as an instrument, a one-standard-deviation increase in 0DTE trading raises volatility by 9.10% of its mean (15.91% of its standard deviation). Even after controlling for market makers' gamma hedging, the effect on volatility remains positive and is driven primarily by speculative retail investors.

Cyclical Consumption and Expected Stock Returns: Evidence from the Korean Capital Market

Youngwoong Won, Chaehwan Won, and Yangkyu Won  2021

Global Business & Finance Review (GBFR), 26(3), 14–32.

We show that a new "cyclical consumption" variable captures consumption risk and predicts expected stock returns, and document where the relation is stronger across KOSPI vs. KOSDAQ and across industries in the Korean capital market.

Determinants of the Prices and Returns of Preferred Stocks

Sehyun Kim, Chaehwan Won, and Youngwoong Won  2020

Asia-Pacific Journal of Business, 11(2), 159–172.

We identify the economic variables that affect prices and returns of preferred stocks. Significant drivers include the consumer sentiment index, CPI, industrial production index, KOSPI volatility index, and the KRW/USD exchange rate.
